
## RF vs Linear Probing for Classification

### Summary

We compared the performance of Random Forest (RF) and Linear Probing (LP) strategies on the same 
foundation models (FM) and k-mer embeddings across 57 classification datasets.

#### Foundation Model Results

Across all 5 foundation models tested, RF and LP show complementary strengths:

- **NTv3 (650M)**: LP shows modest advantage (Δ = +-0.0086 MCC)
- **HyenaDNA**: LP more consistent (Δ = +-0.0934 MCC)
- **DNABERT-2**: LP significantly superior (Δ = +-0.0015 MCC)
- **Caduceus**: Mixed results (Δ = -0.0234 MCC)
- **Evo2 (1B)**: RF advantage on most datasets (Δ = 0.0265 MCC)

Overall, LP outperforms RF across most FM models (total LP wins: 184/284).

#### K-mer Results

K-mer representations show more balanced performance:

- **k=4**: RF mean=0.5170, LP mean=0.4805, Δ=0.0365
- **k=5**: RF mean=0.5190, LP mean=0.5098, Δ=0.0092
- **k=6**: RF mean=0.4999, LP mean=0.5250, Δ=-0.0251

Using the best k-mer for each dataset, we observe similar win distributions (RF: 26, LP: 31).

#### Statistical Significance

Paired t-tests show statistically significant differences for several models:

- HyenaDNA: p=2.90e-08
- Evo2: p=1.39e-02

### Conclusion

RF and LP represent distinct trade-offs: LP fine-tuning generally achieves higher accuracy on average,
while RF provides a simpler, faster alternative with competitive performance on specific models and datasets.
The choice between strategies should be guided by task-specific requirements and computational constraints.
